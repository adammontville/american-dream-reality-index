What this measures

The American Dream Reality Index (ADRI) tracks structural conditions that research links to the plausibility of the American Dream — prosperity and mobility for ordinary people. Higher scores mean conditions are more enabling; lower scores mean they are less. It does not measure individual success or guarantee outcomes.

Ten indicators across six domains: Education, Health, Prosperity, Opportunity, Civic, and Safety. Fixed-anchor 0–100 normalization so that a 5-point change means the same thing in 2028 as in 2015. See the Methodology for the full definition, anchor values, and known limitations.

Read the score carefully

  • The ADRI is backward-looking. Most inputs lag reality by 12–24 months.
  • It is national. It does not describe any state, county, or demographic group.
  • It is a model, not a probability. It does not predict what will happen to any individual.
  • Weights are a design choice. The equal-weight variant is shown alongside so you can see how sensitive the composite is to that choice.
  • Anchor sensitivity is bounded. A univariate perturbation of any single indicator's low or high anchor by ±10% of its span moves the published composite by at most about 1.6 points at any published year, and less than 0.6 points on average. See docs/sensitivity/ for the raw sweep and ranked leverage table.

Update cadence

Full pipeline runs once per year, in Q4 of year t+1, after ACS, income/poverty, life expectancy, and overdose figures are all available for reference year t. Between annual runs, only source revisions and biennial refreshes (NAEP, voter turnout) trigger interim updates. See §6 of the methodology.
